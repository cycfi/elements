Fit

Overview

fit scales its subject uniformly to fill the view, keeping its proportion. Lay a plugin editor out once, in a fixed size, and it scales with the window:

view_.content(
   fit(fixed_size({320, 190}, editor)),
   box(bkd_color)
);

The subject is laid out at its minimum size, its view box, here 320 by 190. The fit sets the view’s scale (see View) so that size fits the view: the smaller of the view’s width over the box’s, and its height over the box’s. In a view 640 by 500, the width decides, and the scale is 2.

Along the other axis there is space over. It is the subject’s, from its left-top up to its maximum size: a fixed size subject stays at the left-top, as above, and one that aligns itself centers, or goes where its alignment puts it:

fit(align_center_middle(fixed_size({320, 190}, editor)))

The fit also asks the view to keep the box’s proportion, its aspect_ratio, which the window keeps while it is resized, where the platform can: a macOS window, or a plugin’s window in a host that honors the proportion it is given. Elsewhere the space over is left empty.

fit is a top-level proxy: it is the view’s content, not inside another element. It sets the scale of the whole view, so what the content opens (menus, tooltips, child windows) scales with it.

Bounded Fit

A plain fit takes any scale, and its own limits are any size: the scale follows it. fit(min_scale, max_scale, subject) keeps the scale within a range. Its limits are the view box times the range, so the window is held between them: a 640 by 400 box fitted from 0.75 to 1.5 keeps the window between 480 by 300 and 960 by 600. In a view larger than the range allows, where a host does not keep to the limits, the scale stops at the top of the range and the space over is left.

view_.content(
   fit(0.75, 1.5, fixed_size({640, 400}, editor)),
   box(bkd_color)
);

The idea is SVG’s: the box is the viewBox, and the fit is preserveAspectRatio with meet, aligned left-top unless the subject aligns itself.

Include

#include <elements/element/fit.hpp>

Declaration

namespace cycfi::elements
{
   template <concepts::Element Subject>
   class fit_element : public proxy<Subject>
   {
   public:

      view_limits             limits(basic_context const& ctx) const override;
      void                    layout(context const& ctx) override;
      void                    prepare_subject(context& ctx) override;
   };

   template <concepts::Element Subject>
   class bounded_fit_element : public fit_element<Subject>
   {
   public:

                              bounded_fit_element(
                                 float min_scale, float max_scale
                               , Subject subject
                              );

      view_limits             limits(basic_context const& ctx) const override;

      float                   min_scale() const;
      float                   max_scale() const;
   };

   template <concepts::Element Subject>
   fit_element<remove_cvref_t<Subject>>
   fit(Subject&& subject);

   template <concepts::Element Subject>
   bounded_fit_element<remove_cvref_t<Subject>>
   fit(float min_scale, float max_scale, Subject&& subject);
}

The rest of each is the Proxy interface (see Proxies).

Expressions

Notation

subject

Instance of Element.

lo, hi

float scales, lo the least.

f

A bounded fit.

Semantics

Expression Semantics

fit(subject)

subject, at its minimum size, scaled uniformly to fill the view; the space over is the subject’s, from its left-top up to its maximum size. The view’s scale and aspect ratio are set to it.

fit(lo, hi, subject)

The same, the scale kept from lo to hi; its limits are the view box times lo to times hi.

f.min_scale(), f.max_scale()

The range.

Example

Ascend Pre’s editor, laid out at 640 by 400 and fitting its window, its scale kept to QPlug’s zoom range:

view_.content(
   fit(zoom_min, zoom_max, fixed_size({view_width, view_height},
      vtile(
         hold(make_header()),
         margin({15, 2, 15, 15},
            htile(
               input,
               margin_left(15, expander)
            )
         )
      )
   )),
   box(bkd_color)
);
